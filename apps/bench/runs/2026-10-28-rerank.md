# 2026-10-28: a real reranker, and real conflicts in GitLab's handbook, at no cost

Step 1 of [the decisive test](../../../docs/design/validation.md#the-decisive-test): the
question check's search alone (`--search-only`) with a real cross-lingual reranker, over the
saved replies of the earlier runs, and a scan of the full edit history of GitLab's travel and
expense pages for real conflicts. No paid model was called. A Chinese copy is in
[2026-10-28-rerank.zh.md](2026-10-28-rerank.zh.md).

## Conclusions

- **With a real reranker, the answer is in what the engine's model reads for 125 of the 135
  questions with an answer, up from 116.**
  - Japanese 40 (35 before), Chinese 40 (37), English 45 (44).
  - The first hit that states the answer is in the top 8 for 106 questions (92 before). The
    first document of the search states it for 112 (100).
  - The mature RAG reads 16 passages from the same search. It has the answer for 128.
- **Search no longer decides the drive.** Both arms now give their model the answer for
  nearly every question. In step 2, a gap between them on the drive measures how each picks
  among values, which is what governance is for.
- **Query translation is not measured yet.** The stand-in translates nothing, and a real
  translation is a paid call, so it waits for step 2. It targets the largest group of the 10
  questions search still misses:
  - 6 Tokaido questions asked in Chinese or Japanese, whose answer only the English article
    states. No hit of the top 100 states it. Long context missed 4 of them too.
  - 2 Chinese questions on the network's length, 1 Japanese question on the hotel cap and 1
    on dry cleaning.
- **The engine leaves out 3 answers the mature RAG keeps.** The engine reads 8 passages and 8
  statements, the mature RAG 16 passages. For one of the 3, the first hit of the engine's own
  wide search states the answer. This is worth a look before step 2.
- **GitLab's handbook holds few real conflicts.** We read every version, since the 2023 move,
  of 16 travel and expense pages: 1,447 versions in three years. They hold 4 real cases
  ([below](#real-conflicts-in-gitlabs-handbook)). The decisive test needs about 80 facts
  that changed or conflict. So step 3 builds the patterns on the handbook's real text. It
  uses real history where there is some, and documented edits for the rest. Each one is
  recorded in the answer key.
- **Reranking on this CPU is slow.**
  - The reranker reads about 1,000 tokens a second on the container's 4 CPUs.
  - Each question's three searches ran at once, so each rerank took a median 24 seconds and
    the run 79 minutes. The bench now runs them one after another, so that the later ones
    read the first one's scores from the cache.
  - These times say nothing about the product, which would rerank on a GPU server. Step 2
    reports rerank time apart from the model's.
- **Cost: none.**

## Real conflicts in GitLab's handbook

We read every version of 16 pages through GitLab's public API: the expense policy, the
corporate cards, accounts payable, spending company money, travel, gifts, benefits,
onboarding and offboarding, visas, recognition, offsites, the remote workspace and the
office equipment pages. A script compared, page by page and over time, the amounts and
periods that sentences on one topic state. These are the real cases:

| Case | Pages | What happened | Pattern it gives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Navan stipend card | accounts payable, the expense policy | On 2026-09-15 accounts payable removed its section on the card "as not valid". The expense policy, last edited on 2026-09-13, still describes the card and its US$1,500 for new team members | Restated and lagging, live today |
| Meals while traveling | the expense policy | Up to US$100 a day until 2026-06-19, then US$120. The commit says it mirrors the internal handbook | A value that changed, after a change elsewhere |
| Lost baggage cover | benefits, travel | US$500 a bag, up to 5 bags, on both pages. Travel dropped it on 2025-04-03. Benefits changed it to US$2,500 a trip, US$1,250 an item and 2 bags on 2026-07-10 | A value that changed, with an older restatement |
| The expense policy itself | the expense policy | Removed from the public handbook on 2026-03-18, back on 2026-05-14 | Retired, then restored |

The scan flagged more, but none was a conflict. Different rules share a topic: team meals
of up to US$100 a person on the corporate card, US$80 a person for team events and client
meals, and recognition meals of US$250.
A gifts line also changed only in its formatting. The handbook is kept with care, which is
why its current pages rarely disagree.

## What ran

- **Engine.** Commit e537951 (PR #64), except that each question's three searches ran at
  once. The change that runs them one after another came after the run started.
- **Embedding.** Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B in 8-bit ONNX, as in the [last search
  run](2026-10-27-search.md). Every question and passage embedding came from the cache.
- **Reranker.** bge-reranker-v2-m3 in 8-bit ONNX (`onnx-community/bge-reranker-v2-m3-ONNX`,
  `q8`), served by `infra/local-embeddings` at `POST /v1/rerank`. Each candidate is cut to
  fit 1,024 tokens with the question. Its score is the sigmoid of the model's logit.
- **Config.** `config/gateway.local-embeddings.yaml`. Translation goes to the stand-in, which
  translates nothing.
- **Commands,** on a fresh stack:

  ```bash
  make clean up migrate
  pnpm bench cache import replies-rerank-partial.jsonl --stack --gateway config/gateway.local-embeddings.yaml
  DEEPSEEK_SEARCH_ONLY_KEY=none pnpm bench ingest examples/company-drive --today 2026-10-27 \
    --gateway config/gateway.local-embeddings.yaml --stack --search-only --json search-qwen3-bge.json
  pnpm bench cache export replies-rerank.jsonl --stack --gateway config/gateway.local-embeddings.yaml
  ```

- **Calls.**
  - The reranker ran 387 times, on 4.68 million tokens: a median 24 seconds, p90 35.
  - 63 more reranks came from the cache of a first attempt. That attempt broke after 12
    minutes, when the server closed a long request. The server now keeps such requests open,
    and the gateway retries twice.
  - All 339 extraction calls and 42 chunk-header calls came from the saved replies. The
    stand-in named the topics.
- **Handbook history.** `GET /projects/42817607/repository/commits?path=...` and the raw file
  at each commit (project `gitlab-com/content-sites/handbook`). It keeps the history from
  before the 2023 move under the new paths.

  - `history.tar.gz`: every version of the 16 pages;
  - `timeline.txt`: the values each page stated over time;
  - the scripts that fetched and compared them.
