# 2026-10-27: first run on DeepSeek

The first run of the engine on a real model, following the plan in
[docs/design/validation.md](../../../docs/design/validation.md): a smoke test on the demo
corpus, then the company drive with 10 of its 150 questions. A Chinese copy is in
[2026-10-27-deepseek.zh.md](2026-10-27-deepseek.zh.md).

## Conclusions

- **The pipeline runs on a real model.** 421 paid calls, no timeouts, no failed extraction
  window; one call's reply failed its schema twice (a topic name over 40 characters).
- **Reading is better than with the stand-ins but below the proposed bar.** Recall 69% (53 of
  77 keyed values), precision 50%; the stand-ins scored 55% and 14%; the bar is 85% and 90%.
  Two engine faults explain most of the gap, and neither is the model's:
  - Postgres rejected the claims of two documents, `pod-lifecycle.html` and its Chinese copy,
    because one claim cited two quotes from the same span (6 of the 20 misses). The in-memory
    store accepts the same batch.
  - The slot `shinkansen_max_operating_speed` takes every line's top speed, because it has no
    line to tell them apart (44 of the 53 values no key gives).

  Without them, recall would be at most 77% and precision about 84%. The misses left are
  mostly the old copies of the expense policy.
- **The prefix cache holds.** The long-context rival sends the whole drive, about 285,000
  tokens, with every question. After the first call, every call got 284,544 of its tokens
  from DeepSeek's cache (99.9%). Over these 10 questions that is 89.9%, the first call
  included; over 150 it would be about 99%. The gate of 90% is met in substance.
- **10 questions do not decide the answering claim.** Right answers: the engine 6, plain
  retrieval 7, a model reading everything 9. Three of the engine's four misses are questions
  whose answer search did not find, and search still runs on the stand-in embeddings and
  reranker.
- **Cost.** The 10-question run cost CNY 2.74 (about US$0.39), reading the whole drive
  included. Extraction is three quarters of it: the model writes about twice the output the
  stand-ins did (1,150 tokens per window), so the dry run's extraction estimate was half the
  real one.

## What ran

- **Commit** `3efc581` on `claude/project-thread-lw1cam`, with the stack (`make up`) on one
  4-vCPU container.
- **Model** `deepseek-flash` (the API lists it as DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, with a context window
  of 1,048,576 tokens). It thinks by default, which bills its reasoning as output; the config
  turns that off with `request: {thinking: {type: disabled}}`.
- **Config** `config/gateway.deepseek.yaml` with `limit: 2` for the day.
- **Prompt versions** `extract_claims@4`, `write_context@1`, `name_topics@2`,
  `answer_question@2`.
- **Commands**, after a dry run of the same command:

  ```bash
  pnpm bench ingest examples/corpus --today 2026-10-27 --gateway <config> --stack
  pnpm bench ingest examples/company-drive --today 2026-10-27 --context 1000000 \
    --gateway <config> --stack --questions 10 --json real10.json
  pnpm bench cache export replies.jsonl --stack
  ```

- **Time**: 12:05 to 12:35 UTC, off-peak by the published hours. Reading the drive took 28
  minutes: 338 windows one after another, 4.5 seconds per call on average and 14.6 at most.
- **Kept** in the project's shared files under `inspiration/validation/runs/2026-10-27/`:
  the 454 replies (`replies.jsonl`, the smoke test's included), the JSON and text reports of
  the dry run and the real run, every metered call (`usage.csv`), the config and the error
  log. The smoke test was run a second time on a fresh database after importing the replies:
  it made no paid call.

## What it cost

DeepSeek bills this account in CNY, and its price page cannot be reached from the
container, so the prices were measured from the balance (`GET /user/balance`) around calls
of known size: CNY 1 per million input tokens, CNY 4 per million output tokens, and, from
this run's total, about CNY 0.02 per million cached input tokens, at 12:00 UTC. At about
7.1 CNY to the US dollar that is half the peak prices in the config, as DeepSeek's off-peak
discount would make it; the peak prices were not measured.

| Task | Calls paid | Input tokens | From the prefix cache | Output tokens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| extraction.extract_claims | 338 | 532,375 | 97,664 | 388,728 |
| retrieval.write_context | 42 | 249,457 | 3,840 | 21,456 |
| page-compiler.name_topics | 11 | 32,018 | 8,064 | 6,346 |
| retrieval.answer_question | 30 | 2,873,346 | 2,563,584 | 2,267 |
| **All** | 421 | 3,687,196 | 2,673,152 | 418,797 |

147 more extraction calls, the retries of the two documents Postgres rejected, came from
the response cache. The balance fell from CNY 109.00 to 106.26 over the run. The whole day,
with the price probes and the smoke test, cost CNY 3.37 (about US$0.47).
