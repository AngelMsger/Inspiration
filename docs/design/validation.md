## Conclusions so far

The first real run, on DeepSeek V4.1 Flash on 2026-10-27, read the company drive, answered its 150 questions three ways and replayed GitLab's expense policy history for CNY 6.61, about US$0.93. Search still ran on the stand-in embedding and reranker. Its run log is `apps/bench/runs/2026-10-27-deepseek.md` (PR #50). A free check then found where answers were lost (`apps/bench/runs/2026-10-27-search.md`), and the second run answered the 150 questions again with a real embedding and whole passages, for CNY 1.46, reading from the first run's saved replies (`apps/bench/runs/2026-10-27-whole.md`, PR #59). On 2026-10-28 the product owner judged the margin over plain RAG too small for what governance costs, and set the bars for the next test ([The decisive test](#the-decisive-test)).

1. **A real model reads far better than the stand-in, and still below the bar.** On the company drive, recall is 77% (59 of 77 keyed values) and precision 53%, against the stand-in's 55% and 14% and a bar of 85% and 90%. One slot takes 44 of the 53 values no key gives: the catalogue offers only the network's top speed (`shinkansen_max_operating_speed`), so the model files every line's top speed under it. Without that slot, recall is 78% and precision 86%. On the GitLab replay, 249 of 294 slot-versions are right (85%), against the stand-in's 174. The misses left are amounts in a sentence that names neither the catalogue's words nor the unit ("Policy is set at $80" under "Phone Service"), a rule with no number, and figures in the older copies and the Shinkansen articles.
2. **With real search, Inspiration answers 116 of 150 right, plain RAG 109 and long context 140.** The first run's 71 (plain RAG 60) was lost in search, before the model saw anything: stand-in vectors that cannot cross languages, a stand-in reranker, and passages cut to snippets of about 80 tokens picked by the words they share with the question. The second run changed only those: Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, run locally for nothing, no reranker, and whole passages. Search now brings the answer for 116 of the 135 questions with one (64 before), and Inspiration is right on 91% of those, as before. By language, Inspiration gets 45 of 50 English questions right, 37 Japanese and 34 Chinese. The run also found two gaps in the answer key (a Japanese date written with its era year, and a Chinese article that states the Chūō opening); corrected from the files and rescored from the saved answers, the three rivals answer 120, 112 and 140.
3. **Over plain RAG, the margin on changed facts is 6 points, near the line we registered as "does not pay for itself in answers".** On the 66 changed facts Inspiration answers 44 and plain RAG 40 (47 and 42 with the corrected key, 8 points); the bar asks 20, and 5 or fewer would mean the governed layer does not pay for itself in answers ([Results that would change the design](#results-that-would-change-the-design)). Real search lifted both nearly equally. What governance still adds is the stale value it keeps out: 6 stale answers against plain RAG's 10, 4% of 150 and within the bar. The margin also proves little either way: on changed facts the two differ on only 8 questions (6 for Inspiration, 2 for plain RAG), a split chance alone gives about 3 times in 10, and the drive's 11 changed facts are too few to measure a gap this size. And plain RAG was not tuned: 9 of its 10 stale answers cite an older copy, which a mature RAG's rule "prefer the governed source, then the newest file" would likely drop. The product owner's verdict (2026-10-28): governance costs people's effort and the system's running, so a few points do not pay for it; Inspiration has to lead a mature RAG by at least 10 points.
4. **On a corpus that fits the window, long context still answers more, by 24 questions instead of 69.** Of the 25 questions it gets and Inspiration misses, 14 are search (8 of the English-only policy asked in Chinese or Japanese, 6 in the long Shinkansen articles), 4 are stale values from a 2023 copy that normalization does not pair with the renamed policy, 4 are the key's gaps, and 3 are the model, given the passage. An Inspiration answer reads about 4,960 tokens, 1.7% of long context's 285,000. But DeepSeek's prefix cache serves long context's corpus at a fiftieth of the price, so on this 311,106-token drive, with one reader and no change between questions, a long-context answer costs about the same, CNY 0.006 against 0.005, and reading the drive once costs Inspiration as much as about 300 such answers. This is the result we registered in advance as "long context beats both on a small corpus". Governance removed no right value: the gap is what search did not bring, since an answer reads 1.7% of the drive. The product owner set the direction on 2026-10-28: a corpus beyond the window is rare in companies, because one topic's material seldom exceeds 1M tokens, while stale and conflicting information is the common problem. So the next test stays within one topic, and Inspiration should answer from everything the asker may read, after governance, whenever it fits the window (governed long context). It can then fall below the raw text only where governance is wrong, which is the owner's principle: organising knowledge must never lose the right value.
5. **Asking meets its count bar; whether the asks were worth it is not yet checked.** After the first read, 20 GitLab versions change 20 facts, and people are asked 7 times (2 todos raised, and open todos given more to decide 5 times): 0.35 asks per changed fact, against a bar of at most 1. The other half of the bar, at least 80% worth asking, needs a reviewer to mark each ask.
6. **The real run found two engine faults, both fixed.** The Postgres store rejected a claim that quotes one span twice (PR #49), and one topic name over 40 characters failed the naming of its whole call (PR #51). Deciding has not yet been scored apart from reading on the real reads.
7. **Real runs are cheap, and the dry run was close.** Off-peak, reading the drive costs CNY 2.37 (about US$0.33) and answering all 150 questions three ways CNY 1.64 (US$0.23): about US$0.56 for a full run, against the dry run's US$0.52. Extraction cost half again more than estimated, because a real model writes about 1,150 tokens per window, twice the stand-in. With the replies kept, the 150-question run reused every reading call and cost CNY 1.33, and the second run, which paid for little but Inspiration's and plain RAG's answers, CNY 1.46 ([Cost, and how it stays low](#cost-and-how-it-stays-low)).
8. **Next: [the decisive test](#the-decisive-test), whose free first step is done** (`apps/bench/runs/2026-10-28-rerank.md`, PR #64). With a real reranker, bge-reranker-v2-m3 run locally, the answer is in what Inspiration's model reads for 125 of the 135 questions with one (116 before), and in a mature RAG's 16 passages for 128. So search no longer decides the drive, and a gap there measures governance. Every version since 2023 of 16 GitLab travel and expense pages, 1,447 in all, holds only 4 real conflicts, so the test's cases are built on the handbook's real text. Then the drive with the mature RAG and governed long context (about CNY 5), and the two topics on deepseek-flash and on a faster, cheaper model (about CNY 18). Still on the list: pairing a renamed copy with its document as an older edition (the 4 stale answers), a top-speed slot per line, re-reading less when only headings change, a reviewer's verdict on the replay's 7 asks, and 3 answers the mature RAG's passages hold and Inspiration's sources leave out.

## What we must prove

Inspiration is worth building if it does three things on the documents a company already has. It gives readers and agents the value that holds today, with the passage behind it. It does that more often than search over the same documents. And it asks people only about what the documents cannot settle.

Those documents repeat each other, contradict each other, lag in translation and change without notice. Every test below runs on real public documents of that kind, never on sentences we wrote. The business layer, accounts and permissions are left out: they are standard and certainly buildable.

## Answer keys written by hand before any run

A result means something only if the right answer was fixed before the run, from the documents themselves. The GitLab timeline, the Kubernetes answers and the company-as-it-looks key (PR #34) work this way. That key has 36 facts and 114 entries, each with its file, revision and verbatim quote, and a test checks every quote and revision. Its 150 questions are 50 per language: 69 about unchanged facts, 66 about changed ones (two phrasings per language) and 15 that the documents in use do not answer.

1. **Read from the documents.** Every expected value names its file, revision and the quoted passage.
2. **A value per date.** Each fact gets its value from each date it changed, so "what is the hotel cap on 2023-09-01?" has one right answer.
3. **A second reader.** Someone other than the author checks 20 random entries, and every disagreement is settled and recorded. Our first key already needed this: it said GitLab's phone cap stopped being stated in August 2023, but the page still states it under its Phone Service heading ($80, then $100 from 2026-06-19). We propose the product owner or a teammate does this; it takes about 30 minutes.
4. **The question set.** About 150 questions, a third each in English, Chinese and Japanese. Half ask about facts that never changed, half about facts that changed, conflict or appear only in a lagging translation. One in ten has "not stated" as its right answer, to catch invented values.
5. **Frozen.** A key is never edited to match a result. When a document changes, the key is written again and old runs are kept as they were. When a run shows the key missed what a file says, the key is corrected from the file, the correction is listed in the corpus's README, and the run keeps the score it had.
6. **Scored by rule.** A value matches when it equals the key after units are normalized. Where a model judges a free-text answer, it judges only against the key, and a sample of its judgements is checked by hand. A right answer can still mention an old value beside the new one, so the judge also checks whether it hedges between them.

## The baseline: plain RAG on the same documents

The value of the knowledge layer shows only against what people use today. So every question runs three times: through Inspiration and through two rivals, with the same documents and model. The engine thread builds the rival runs and scores all three side by side.

- **Plain RAG.** The same retrieval with statements and governance turned off: passages are retrieved and the model answers from them with citations. This is what most knowledge products do, RAGFlow among them. It is a hybrid, but untuned: 400 to 800-token chunks with a contextual header each, a multilingual embedding, BM25, reciprocal rank fusion of the 50 best from each, and the 8 best passages read whole; no reranker, no query translation, and every copy found is read.
- **Mature RAG** (from [the decisive test](#the-decisive-test)). Plain RAG plus what a carefully built RAG has: a cross-lingual reranker, the question translated into the corpus's languages, 12 to 16 passages, and the rule "prefer the governed source, then the newest file". Inspiration gets the same search, so only governance differs.
- **Long context, where it fits.** For a small corpus, the model reads every document with its date and answers. It is the strongest simple rival, and on both real runs it won at the drive's size (conclusion 4). Where Inspiration cannot beat it on answers, the layer has to earn its place on what does not fit one window, on cost, on change alerts and on owners' work instead.
- **Governed long context, where it fits** (from the decisive test). Everything the asker may read after governance, replaced editions left out and outdated values marked. It should never be less right than raw long context; where it is, governance has lost a right value.

All three runs report the same numbers as claim 3, plus tokens and time per answer. Whatever Inspiration wins is what reading once, governing and keeping history are worth.

## What the real documents have already shown

Reading is still the open risk, and search now decides most answers. The runs on real text, first with the stand-in model and then with DeepSeek, taught these things invented sentences never would have.

- **Real text is hard to read.** The stand-in missed "$10K", amounts in table cells, HTML tables, a rule with no number ("receipts are required for all expenses" means a threshold of 0), numbers in words ("up to three minor versions older") and another unit ("5分" in Japanese). These are the cases a real model must get right.
- **Real text found engine bugs.** A line break in the HTML split a sentence, "…)," ended one, and a long real heading made the gateway refuse every topic name in an answer. All three are fixed.
- **The baseline and the todo count found two more.** Statements were crowding the answer's own passages out of its sources; an answer now reads 8 passages plus 8 statements. And a fact stated in two places raised a todo when one of the places was removed. Both are fixed.
- **Real text found two missing rules, now built.** A fact a page stopped stating used to vanish without notice (20 of GitLab's fact-versions); it is now kept and marked, with one todo. Copies and translations used to count as separate sources; they now count once.
- **The company-as-it-looks corpus found one more fix and one gap, now closed.** A value that only a reference copy states, older than the governed document, is now out of date: the 2021 copy's 30 days no longer answers beside the policy's 90, while plain RAG still answers 30. The gap: an old revision whose fact has no governing slot was read as current, as with the Chuo Shinkansen's opening in the 2024 Shinkansen articles. PR #38 closed it by recognising old copies and revisions of one document and answering from the latest, which cut Inspiration's stale answers from 5 to 3.
- **Old translations leave out rather than contradict.** The Japanese Kubernetes pages from 2023 and 2024 state the same values as the English ones, and simply lack the newer sections.
- **A real model reads more, and writes more.** It finds 3,241 statements on the drive where the stand-in found 1,339, writes about 1,150 tokens per extraction window, twice the stand-in, and leaves 26 conflict cases where the stand-in left 135.
- **A slot drawn too wide costs precision.** The catalogue's one top speed for the whole network collected every line's top speed: 44 of the 53 values no key gives. The fix is in the catalogue, not the model.
- **Real output found two engine faults.** A model quoted one span twice for one claim, which the Postgres store rejected (PR #49), and one topic name over 40 characters failed the naming of its whole call (PR #51). Both are fixed.
- **Re-reading follows the edit.** On the GitLab replay, each version reads only its added and changed blocks: 448 windows over 21 versions, where reading each version in full would take about 1,000. The one over-read is a renamed title with re-levelled sections, which re-reads every block under them, because extraction reads a block with its headings (2023-10-05: 49 windows for a 28% edit).
- **DeepSeek's prefix cache holds.** After the first question, 99.9% of each long-context call came from it, as the dry run assumed.
- **Snippets lost answers search had found.** A passage used to reach the answer as about 80 tokens picked by the words it shares with the question, so across languages, or worded differently, the snippet left the answer out. Read whole, with a real embedding, the answer is within reach for 116 of 135 questions instead of 64, at about 4,960 input tokens an answer instead of 1,180.
- **The answer key needs its second reader.** The second run's answers showed two places the key missed: a date written with its era year inside ("1964年（昭和39年）10月1日") and a Chinese article that states the Chūō opening. Both are corrected and recorded (rule 5).

## The decisive test

Proposed 2026-10-28, to the bars the product owner set that day. It asks one question: when information is stale, conflicting or distorted, does Inspiration lead a mature RAG by at least 10 points, on a fast, cheap model as well as on a strong one? The company drive cannot answer it. Its versions are dated and in order, the newest file always holds, the policy writes its own history ("previously $80"), and only 11 facts changed. There a rule as simple as "prefer the governed source, then the newest file" does most of governance's work.

**Corpus.** Two topics of real public text, each well under 1M tokens, since one topic's material in a company seldom exceeds that. Travel and expenses comes from GitLab's handbook: 4,195 pages at main, with the public history of every edit. It already holds real restatements, such as the Navan expense guide repeating the expense rules, and real older versions, such as the legacy contract pages. Its text is English, asked in all three languages. One area of the Kubernetes docs brings real lagging translations in Chinese and Japanese, and changes between releases. The free scan of step 1 found that its current pages rarely disagree: every version since 2023 of 16 travel and expense pages, 1,447 in all, holds 4 real cases. A card retired on one page is still described in the policy, two values changed on dated edits, and the policy page was removed and restored. So each pattern below is built on the real text, from its real history where it has one and by a documented edit otherwise, and gets at least 10 facts.

| Pattern | How it is built | The simple rule it defeats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Restated and lagging | a team page or guide restates a policy value; the policy changed later; the page was edited since for another reason | newest file wins |
| Newer but not authoritative | a personal copy or draft saved after the policy, with a proposed value | newest file wins |
| Renamed or moved | the policy under its old title or path (GitLab moved its handbook in 2023) | near-duplicate detection |
| Lagging translation | a Chinese or Japanese page edited recently, its content from an older English revision | newest file, governed source |
| Governed sources disagree | two current governed pages give different values, neither replacing the other | any rule that picks one: a right answer names both |
| Changed elsewhere | an announcement, changelog or release note changes a value the page still states | governed source wins |
| Distorted restatement | a summary rounds a value or drops its condition | governed source wins, when the summary is governed too |

**Arms**, each on the same documents and model:

| Arm | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Mature RAG | today's search, plus a cross-lingual reranker, query translation, 12 to 16 passages, and "prefer the governed source, then the newest file" |
| Inspiration | the same search, plus statements and governance |
| Raw long context | the topic's documents, where the model's window holds them |
| Governed long context | the topic after governance, where it fits |

**Models.** `deepseek-flash` with thinking off, as before, and a faster, cheaper, somewhat weaker model, because response speed is a product advantage too. The product owner picks the second. Long context runs only where a model's window holds the topic.

**Measures.** Right; stale; silent conflict, one value given where governed sources disagree, without a word of the other; cost per answer and of reading; latency per answer, median and p90; review load, as todos per 100 documents and how many "Decide for me" settles; and evidence retention, audited offline (bar 1). Today an answer of about 4,900 input tokens on `deepseek-flash` takes a median 1.3 seconds and a p90 of 1.8; long context's latency was not recorded.

**Pass bars**, fixed before any paid call:

1. Governance removes no right value. An offline audit checks this apart from the answers: for each keyed fact, whether what governance keeps still holds its value, its conditions and who may read it. Governed long context should also be at least as right as raw long context on every pattern. A lower score there shows where to look but proves no loss by itself, and an equal score proves no retention, since prompts, order and the model move answers too (the product owner's review of PR #63). If the audit finds a loss, it is fixed first.
2. On each model, Inspiration answers at least 10 points more questions right than the mature RAG, and no fewer on the unchanged control. This bar is the product owner's. If it is missed, governance does not pay for its cost: keep a light layer of editions, dates and conflict notes on a mature RAG, and drop statement-level governance.
3. On the fast model, Inspiration is at least as right as the mature RAG on `deepseek-flash`, with a lower median latency. Proposed; the product owner sets it.
4. Review load is at most 5 todos per 100 documents after "Decide for me". Proposed; the product owner sets it.

**Size.** About 80 changed or conflicting facts and about 30 unchanged ones as a control, each asked once in English, Chinese and Japanese: about 330 questions. A 15-point gap needs about 80 such facts to show reliably; the drive has 11. Gaps are reported with 95% intervals, resampling facts rather than questions.

**Steps and cost**, at the off-peak prices measured in the first run:

| 1. **Done 2026-10-28.** A reranker on the drive, checked with `--search-only`; a scan of the handbook for real conflicts | search recovers 9 answers: 125 of 135 within reach, 128 for the mature RAG; the handbook holds 4 real conflicts, too few, so the cases are built | nothing; query translation moves to step 2, under CNY 0.1 |
| 2. The drive with the mature RAG, Inspiration on the stronger search and governed long context; the rest from saved replies | whether the 6 points survive a mature baseline; bar 1 on the tidy case | about CNY 5 |
| 3. The two topics and their key, with a second reader for the key | | nothing; a few days |
| 4. The two topics on `deepseek-flash`: reading, then the four arms, with latency | bars 1, 2 and 4: the decision | about CNY 18 |
| 5. The same questions on the fast model | bars 2 and 3 | nothing on a local model; an API model is priced once chosen |

A test beyond the window was dropped: the product owner judged it rare in companies, and it would have cost about CNY 20 more.

2. **Real runs on DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (two runs done, 2026-10-27).** The model goes through the model gateway, with a multilingual embedding beside it, because the stand-in search cannot match a Chinese or Japanese question to English text. The first run read the drive, answered all 150 questions and replayed GitLab's history for CNY 6.61, about US$0.93, and the response cache replays whatever a change does not touch ([Cost, and how it stays low](#cost-and-how-it-stays-low)). Gate: claims 1, 3 and 5 have real numbers. Met: the second run answered on a real embedding, Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B run locally, for CNY 1.46.
3. **Fix and run again.** Work on prompts, the fact vocabulary and tables, then rerun the same keys. Before a reading result changes the design, reading runs once on a larger model, for a few dollars, to tell a limit of Flash from a limit of the design. It started with the embedding and whole passages (the second run) and goes on with [the decisive test](#the-decisive-test) (conclusion 8). Gate: the pass bars are met, or a result from [Results that would change the design](#results-that-would-change-the-design) changes the design.
4. **A design partner's documents.** A company's own documents, ideally written in Chinese or Japanese, run the same way with their permission. Gate: one of their owners marks which todos were worth asking (claim 4).

### Where each run is kept

Every paid run leaves a trace that a later evaluation can reuse, so nothing is paid for twice. A run log in `apps/bench/runs/` (the first is `2026-10-27-deepseek.md`, PR #50) records the commits, commands, prompt versions, costs and results. The raw reply of every call is exported with `pnpm bench cache export` to the project's shared files, beside each run's reports and every metered call. `pnpm bench cache import` loads the replies into a fresh stack, and a rerun then pays only for the calls a change touches.

### Cost, and how it stays low

A full run costs about US$0.56 off-peak on DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, so the loop can turn several times a day. The dry run (PR #39) estimated US$0.52 before any paid call: it sends every call of the stand-in run through the gateway with the inputs a real model would get, and prices them at DeepSeek's rates with a prefix cache like DeepSeek's. Its output lengths are the stand-ins', so it underestimates extraction, where a real model writes twice as much.

The model is `deepseek-flash` (listed as DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, with a window of 1,048,576 tokens, so the 311,106-token corpus fits whole). It thinks by default, which bills its reasoning as output, so the config turns thinking off. DeepSeek bills this account in CNY, and its price page cannot be reached from our environment, so the first run measured the prices from the balance around calls of known size, off-peak at 12:00 UTC: CNY 1 per million input tokens, CNY 0.02 per million served from DeepSeek's cache, and CNY 4 per million output tokens. At about 7.1 CNY to the dollar that is half the reported peak prices of US$0.30, 0.006 and 1.20, as the off-peak discount would make it. Peak is seven hours on weekdays, 01:00 to 04:00 and 06:00 to 10:00 UTC; its prices were not measured.

Reading the drive costs CNY 2.37 however many questions are asked, and each question adds about CNY 0.011 across the three rivals, three quarters of it long context. Since the second run an answer reads whole passages, about 4,960 input tokens instead of 1,180, so Inspiration's and plain RAG's answers cost about CNY 1.35 for 150 questions instead of CNY 0.40, and a full run about CNY 4.96. Embeddings are not in the figure: Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B runs locally for nothing, and embedding the drive takes about 27 minutes on the container's 4 CPUs. There is no reranker until a real one scores whole passages. The GitLab replay for claim 4 runs separately; it cost CNY 1.91.

Four things keep runs cheap:

1. **The response cache.** The gateway answers a call whose model, prompt, input and settings are unchanged from its cache, at no cost, and keeps replies in Valkey for 30 days; the export above keeps them longer. So a change pays only for what it touches: a new answer prompt pays for the answers (CNY 1.64 for all 150 questions), not for reading again.
2. **Corpus first in long context.** The long-context rival puts the documents before the question, so DeepSeek's cache serves the corpus from the second question on. With the question first, every call would pay for the whole 285,000 tokens it sends, about CNY 43 for 150 questions instead of CNY 1.24. The question after long documents is also the usual advice for answer quality, so the change does not weaken the rival.
3. **Off-peak.** Full runs are scheduled outside DeepSeek's weekday peak hours, at half price.
4. **Spending limits.** The prepaid balance is the hard cap: the provider stops everything when it runs out. Inside it, one gateway budget of US$5 a day covers all calls together. On a database it counts the day's earlier runs, and dry runs never count. The budget is a soft stop. The gateway refuses a call once the day's spend, plus what calls in flight in the same process hold, reaches the limit. A call admitted under the limit can still cost more than it held, through a schema retry or a dearer fallback model, and runs in other processes count only once they have metered. So a day may exceed US$5, and nothing but the prepaid balance bounds by how much.

**Reading is checked in full.** Reading every window costs about CNY 2, no more than a sample of windows would, so there is no sample. The reading check scores the whole read against the answer key without calling a model: for each place the key gives a value, whether governance holds that value (found), other values only (misread) or nothing (missed), plus the values the key does not give (extra), which give precision. The 12 facts without a catalogue entry are reported apart, because only answers can find them.

**Slot fits are held to the catalogue.** Extraction holds a model's slot fits to the fact catalogue: each value has one written form, and a fit with a wrong unit or an unknown qualifier loses its slot and is counted. Without this, "$300" and "300 USD" would be two values and a false conflict. On the first real run, 8 of 3,241 statements were kept without their slot.

The public data needs no sensitivity clearance, so DeepSeek's own API may read it. A design partner's documents in step 4 go only to a provider the gateway clears for their sensitivity; DeepSeek publishes the model's weights, so a host the partner trusts can serve the same model.

## Results that would change the design

A validation is worth running only if some results would change what we build. These were proposed before any result, so that no result can be explained away afterwards. The last column is what the real runs showed.

| If we see | It means | We change | Real runs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reading recall under 70% after prompt work | Fact slots are too rigid for real wording | Keep slots for high-value facts (amounts, limits, deadlines, owners); keep the rest as open statements grouped by meaning, governed less strictly | Not seen: 77% before any prompt work |
| Plain RAG within 5 points of us on changed facts | The governed layer does not pay for itself in answers | Lead with change alerts and owner reviews; make extract-on-use the default; answer with RAG checked against known statements | **Nearly seen:** 6 points ahead with real search (44 against 40 of 66), 8 with the key corrected; 11 on the stand-in search. The product owner judged it too small and replaced the row with the next one |
| Under 10 points ahead of a mature RAG on the decisive test (bar set by the product owner, 2026-10-28) | Statement-level governance does not pay for its cost | Keep a light layer (editions, dates, conflict notes) on a mature RAG or on long context, and drop statement-level governance | Not run yet |
| Governed long context less right than raw long context | Governance removed or hid a right value | Fixed before anything else runs | Not run yet; in the second run governance removed no right value |
| Long context beats both on a small corpus | A model that reads everything is enough when everything fits | Aim at what no model can read at once (many documents, years of history), and say so in the product | **Seen:** 140 of 150 right, against 71 and 60, and 116 and 109 with real search.
